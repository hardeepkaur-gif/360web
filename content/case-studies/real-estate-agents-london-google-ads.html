The Challenge

Six Services, One Generic Funnel

Before this initiative, every service (rent guarantees, property management, lettings and more) fought for visibility on the same broad pages, making it impossible to separate genuine leads from false ones.

  • A landlord searching for a rent guarantee service would never land on a dedicated page
  • Enquiries were not separated from job applications and marketing calls
  • No figures were available to prove whether each service line deserved its advertising spend

Our Approach

Split Every Service Onto Its Own Dedicated Page

Instead of one central website for every audience, we built an individual property for each service line, each designed, worded and targeted around one distinct customer intention.

Eight Dedicated Websites

  • Created eight independent websites, each focused on one unique offer

Monthly Targeting & Exclusions

  • Optimised search targeting and exclusions every month based on lead results

Manual Enquiry Qualification

  • Recorded and sorted every enquiry manually across all properties

Budget Shifted to Winners

  • Gradually increased spend on the two most profitable service lines

The Results

Quality Overtook Volume as the Real Measure of Success

Once each property had its own dedicated function, genuine leads started to stand out from the noise. The top performers in the portfolio now comfortably beat the original shared page.

  • One property converted at an impressive 78.3%: 54 of 69 enquiries were genuine leads
  • An instruction came from an RTM company managing 26 flats nearby
  • Another property turned 105 enquiries into 52 genuine leads
  • The flagship shared site converts at 35.1%, the lowest-performing core property
  • Four new specialist pages have already reached an 83.3% conversion rate
  • Nearly half of all genuine leads came from the two top services

Why This Worked

One Page, One Intent, One Clear Answer

Eight months of live campaign data exposed the real problem, and it wasn’t traffic. The site was never short on clicks. It was short on relevance. A landlord chasing a guaranteed rent scheme and a tenant hunting for a flat were both funnelled to the same generic homepage, built to convince nobody in particular. That’s why the fix wasn’t a bigger budget or sharper bidding; it was structural.

Give every intent its own page, speaking directly to that one person’s problem, and the noise falls away on its own. Any business running several services off one homepage is likely sitting on the same fix. Split the intents apart, and the site starts doing the qualifying work the sales team used to do by hand.

The Challenge

Competing in a Crowded, Price-Sensitive Category

Dental instruments are a tough category to sell online: buyers search using precise clinical terms rather than brand names, and generic listings get buried among near-identical competitors. Four problems needed solving at once:

  • Making hundreds of SKUs discoverable by exact clinical search terms
  • Building buyer trust where an unbranded listing looks interchangeable
  • Reducing dependency on a single marketplace channel
  • Managing a large low-priced catalogue without manual, one-off listing work

Our Approach

A Four-Phase Build Across Marketplace and Owned Channels

The programme covered four connected phases — from eBay storefront foundations through Shopify and ongoing growth:

eBay Storefront Build & Branding

  • Designed a branded storefront with header, banner, and featured-category modules
  • Built ~49 custom store categories mapped to how clinicians actually search
  • Configured consistent business policies for postage, returns, and payment

Listing Creation & Marketplace SEO

  • Built keyword-led titles and complete item specifics for each listing
  • Standardised mobile-first description templates across the catalogue
  • Added multi-pack variants and bulk listing workflows to scale efficiently

Shopify E-Commerce Website

  • Built a brand-led Shopify site (virco.co.uk) with 8 primary collections and 65 subcategories
  • Published 319 products (382 variants) with filtering and sorting
  • Enabled multi-currency checkout across 237 countries with cards, Apple Pay, PayPal, Klarna, and more

Ongoing Management & Marketing

  • Ran promotional campaigns (e.g. “VIRCO20”) to lift average basket value
  • Continuously optimised listings, re-priced against competitors, and expanded into new categories
  • Managed orders, enquiries, and returns while protecting seller performance standards

Why This Worked

Two Channels, Two Different Jobs

The temptation with marketplace selling is to treat eBay and a new website as the same problem solved twice. But eBay is a discovery engine; buyers are already searching for the exact product, so the job is to be found and look credible fast. A website is a brand engine; there’s no competing listing on the page, so the job is building trust and protecting margin over time.

Rather than splitting effort evenly, the programme built eBay’s discovery machinery first: category structure, keyword-led listings, bulk workflows, because that’s where the volume-driven catalogue could earn trust fastest. Only once that engine was proven did they carry the same category logic over to build an owned Shopify channel. The transferable principle: don’t build every channel to do the same job; build each channel to do the one job it’s actually good at, and let the proof from one carry over to the next.

The Challenge

Serving Two Very Different Buyers on One Homepage

A first-time buyer and a fleet operator often land on the same page. One needs to understand what a water-fed pole even is; the other needs a payload figure and a crash-test certificate. The site had to serve both, without either feeling patronised or lost, while also handling genuinely complex, configurable products across a growing catalogue.

Our Approach

Four Disciplines, Built to Work as One System

Brand Identity

  • Built a disciplined single-red, single-typeface system (Century Gothic) applied consistently from homepage to checkout, with an SVG-first logo and documented type scale

Content Strategy

  • Mapped content to the real buying cycle: four homepage routes (Poles, Systems, Parts, Support), range pages organised by buyer constraint, and a post-purchase content engine (maintenance videos, FAQs, datasheets) that drives re-orders

Technical SEO

  • Built a three-click architecture, hand-written titles/descriptions, a connected schema graph, a ten-part sitemap index, and full-page caching for an 8.7× speed gain

Web Development

  • Delivered a WooCommerce build with dependent product configuration (length, fitting, brush head), trade pricing conventions, PayPal Pay Later, LiveChat, and a custom trade-account and quote system

The Results

A Digital Estate Built to Compound

  • 130+ pages unified under one consistent brand system
  • 72 products across a fully structured, three-level shop taxonomy
  • 8.7× faster page delivery through full-page caching: 469ms vs 4,056ms
  • 83% reduction in homepage payload size (528KB → 88KB) for faster load on any connection
  • A connected schema graph and 10-part sitemap index giving the site a crawlable, unambiguous structure to compete against larger distributors

Why This Worked

Letting the Buyer Sort Themselves

A specialist manufacturer’s site often tries to do too much on one page, cramming in beginner explanations and technical specs side by side, hoping everyone finds what they need. Brodex had exactly this risk: a first-time buyer needing the basics and a fleet operator needing a payload figure were landing on the same homepage.

Rather than writing one page to serve both, the site was structured around four clear entry points. So each visitor’s first click did the sorting for them; nobody had to read past all the questions to get to theirs. One disciplined brand system held that structure together, so no matter which path a visitor took, it still felt like one company. The takeaway for any business with more than one type of buyer: don’t write a compromise page, build clear paths, and let people choose the one that’s actually theirs.
